## Honest status

Anlingo is an AI writing assistant and English coach that I built myself, with AI
coding agents. Both the marketing site and the web app respond online. The
product is pre-launch: I have no user, subscriber or revenue figure, so this page
does not invent any, and I do not claim that a payment has ever gone through.

The last commits are from July 2026 (18 July for web and API, 22 July for the
mobile app). The Flutter companion app has not been released to any store. It is
in development, and some of its work is not pushed yet.

Writing you paste into Anlingo is processed by third-party AI providers, and
history is stored. I describe it as an honest cloud AI coach, not a private one.

## Hard problems

<DecisionCard
  title="Racing AI providers for one answer"
  subtitle="Hedged multi-provider grammar routing"
  problem="A single AI provider is sometimes slow, returns malformed output, or is down, and a grammar check that waits on it feels broken."
  decision="The grammar route starts up to three providers in a staggered way (Gemini on Vertex AI and AI Studio, OpenRouter, NVIDIA, DeepSeek) and accepts the first answer that passes strict JSON validation. Malformed output counts as a failure. If every provider fails, a deterministic rule-based checker still returns a result."
  result="A slow or wrong provider cannot block the user. A slow loser may still be billed, and I have no cost data, so I do not claim savings. Other routes are not all multi-provider."
/>

<DecisionCard
  title="Bounded AI cost and abuse"
  subtitle="Concurrency, timeouts, kill switches, tiers"
  problem="AI calls cost money, and a free product invites abuse."
  decision="A guard caps concurrent in-flight provider calls and applies per-provider timeouts. Kill switches can turn off an area, prompts are size-capped, and per-user and per-IP hourly caps run in Redis. Usage tiers enforce guest, free and premium limits, and an allowance is refunded when a call fails."
  result="Cost has a ceiling by design. The numbers are internal and I have no measured spend."
/>

<DecisionCard
  title="Billing that survives retries"
  subtitle="Verified webhooks, idempotent updates"
  problem="Payment providers retry and reorder webhooks, and a wrong subscription state either locks out a paying user or gives away the product."
  decision="Stripe and Whop webhook signatures are verified on the raw request body, MoMo notifications are checked with a timing-safe signature, and Whop deliveries are de-duplicated with a Firestore transaction. Mobile billing goes through RevenueCat."
  result="The billing code and its tests exist. Whether a real payment has been processed is not verified, and the advertised Teams plan has no billing yet."
/>

<DecisionCard
  title="Copy that cannot drift from code"
  subtitle="Content-accuracy contract tests"
  problem="A July audit of my own pages found public copy that contradicted what the product does, including usage limits and a refund promise."
  decision="Contract tests for the landing and web apps fail the build when copy claims drift from the code. The fixes from that audit are in the working tree and are not committed yet."
  result="This is the most useful lesson of the project. The claims on this page were checked against the code the same way."
/>

## System Design Notes

<Callout variant="info" title="Verified technology stack">
  A pnpm and Turborepo monorepo (web app on Next.js 15, landing site on Next.js 16, admin app, and an Express and TypeScript API), plus a separate Flutter repository. Firebase Auth for sign-in. Firestore is the primary store, Redis handles caching and rate limits, and PostgreSQL is only an optional history archive. Speech uses Google Cloud Speech and Text-to-Speech. Billing code covers Stripe, Whop, MoMo and RevenueCat. Sentry and PostHog for errors and analytics. The web apps deploy on Netlify and the API runs as a Docker container on Dokploy behind Traefik.
</Callout>

<DecisionCard
  title="One backend, three clients"
  subtitle="Web, admin and Flutter share one API"
  problem="Three surfaces would otherwise each grow their own copy of the rules."
  decision="The web app proxies API calls to one Express service, the Flutter app calls the same routes through Dio, and shared Zod contracts describe the learning features."
  result="Usage limits, billing state and history have a single source of truth."
/>

<DecisionCard
  title="Product depth beyond grammar"
  subtitle="Notebook, IELTS writing, speaking tutor"
  problem="Fixing a sentence once does not teach the writer anything."
  decision="A Mistake Notebook stores recurring errors, an IELTS writing evaluation scores essays, and a speaking tutor runs turn-based attempts."
  result="These exist in the code. How well the IELTS scoring matches real examiners is not verified."
/>

## What I would take from this

Race your providers instead of trusting one. Put a ceiling on anything that costs
money per request. And write tests that fail when your marketing copy lies.
