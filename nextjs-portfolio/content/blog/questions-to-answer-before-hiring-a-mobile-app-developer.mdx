Before you hire a mobile app developer, answer 12 questions on paper: who the app is for, what gets built first, which platforms and data it needs, and who maintains it after launch. If you can answer most of them in one clear sentence each, you are ready for a quote.

Most mobile app projects go wrong before the first line of code. Not because the developer is bad, but because nobody wrote down what the app is, who it is for and what happens on launch day. I build mobile apps in Flutter and React Native, and web apps in Next.js, and these are the questions I want answered before anyone quotes a price.

Fill them in alone. It takes about 30 minutes and saves a week of back and forth. If you prefer a printable version, the [free one-page checklist](/checklist) has all 12.

## Part 1: Who the app is for and why

**1. Who is the app for?** Describe one real person, not "everyone". What do they do in a normal day? A vague audience produces a vague app, and a vague app produces a vague quote.

**2. What problem does it solve for them?** One sentence, with no feature names. If the sentence needs the word "and" three times, you have three products.

**3. What happens in the first 60 seconds?** What does a new user see, tap and get? If you cannot describe it, the app is not scoped yet.

**4. How will you know it worked?** Pick one number: sign-ups, bookings, repeat users or revenue. One number keeps every later decision honest.

## Part 2: What gets built

**5. What is the smallest version you would be happy to launch?** List at most five features. Everything else is version 2. This single answer changes the price and the timeline more than any other.

**6. Which platforms first: iPhone, Android or both?** Say why. "Both" is allowed, but it needs a reason, such as your users being split evenly. If you do not know, say so, and ask your developer to explain the trade-off.

**7. Do users need accounts?** If yes, decide how they sign in: email, phone, Apple or Google. Sign-in choices affect both the design and the store review.

**8. What data does it store, and who can see whose data?** This is where most apps get it wrong. Decide early which user can see which record, because it shapes the whole backend.

**9. What does it connect to?** Payments, maps, notifications, WhatsApp, your existing website or systems. Every connection adds work and something that can break.

## Part 3: After launch

**10. Who maintains it next year?** Bug fixes, store updates, new phone versions and server costs do not stop at launch. Decide who owns them before you start.

**11. Who handles store review and support?** Apple and Google review every release, and someone must answer user questions. If paid features are involved, the store rules on payments matter early, not at the end.

**12. What is your budget range and your deadline?** And which one matters more if you must choose? A developer can only help you trade scope against time if you tell them which side you would give up.

## How to read your answers

- **9 or more clear answers:** you are ready to ask for a quote.
- **5 to 8 clear answers:** you are ready for a scoping call. Bring your answers.
- **Fewer than 5:** spend one day on questions 1 and 2. They unlock the rest.

## Red flags in a quote

Watch for a price with no questions asked first, for "both platforms, no problem" with no reason given, and for a quote that says nothing about launch, maintenance or who owns the code. A good quote starts from your answers to the questions above.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should answering the 12 questions take?

About 30 minutes if you work alone. If a question takes longer, that is useful: it shows what to discuss with your developer first.

### Do I need to know whether to use Flutter or React Native?

No. You need to describe your app, your users and your team. The framework follows from those answers. I explain how I decide in [Flutter or React Native? How I choose for a new app](/blog/flutter-or-react-native-how-to-choose).

### What should I look for beyond the quote?

Communication and proof of earlier work matter as much as price. My guide on [how to choose a reliable freelance developer](/blog/choose-reliable-freelance-developer) covers how to check both.

### How much will my app cost and how long will it take?

It depends on your answer to question 5, the smallest version you would launch. For web projects, the same logic is in [how long it takes to build a website](/blog/how-long-to-build-website) and [what a small business website costs](/blog/small-business-website-cost).
